What the course is

CAT 402 / CAT 402W is called Professional and Technopreneurship Development. A technopreneur is the course’s word for someone who starts a business around IT. The synopsis says the course has two parts.

1. Technopreneurship development

Entrepreneurship through IT-based products and services, covering the whole path from defining the business idea to writing the business plan. Students are encouraged to join the annual business plan competition.

2. Professional development

Issues IT professionals must understand: ethics, social implications, professionalism, intellectual property, cyber laws, freedom of speech and privacy. It also covers careers: job applications, interviews, career paths and graduate studies.

Workshops and seminars with successful technopreneurs are planned. They talk about developing ideas, launching and running a business, and creating marketing strategies that last.

The worry behind the lecture

The lecture opens with a poll: if you graduate tomorrow, what worries you most? Finding a job, having enough skills, competing with AI, choosing a career, or starting something on your own. The slide says the point is not to become an entrepreneur. The real question is: how do I stay valuable when technology changes? Entrepreneurship is one possible path, and the ability to adapt is needed on all paths.

How the graduate career model changed

Before

Degree → job → promotion → career.

Now

Degree → skills → portfolio → opportunities → continuous learning. One person can have several income or career paths.

Professional development is not just attending courses. It means steadily improving knowledge, skills, communication, judgement and adaptability, so you become more useful over time. What makes someone employable is usually a mix (degree, CGPA, technical skill, communication, experience, portfolio, problem solving) and the mix changes by job. Employers increasingly ask “What can you do? What problem can you solve? Can you learn quickly?”, not only “What did you study?”

GenAI: where human value stays

GenAI can already help with writing, research, coding, data, design, marketing and analysis. So “I can use AI” is not the valuable skill. The valuable skill is knowing what to ask, check, improve and apply.

GenAI isHumans bring
Fast, scalable, pattern-based, good at draftsContext, judgement, responsibility, trust, relationships

The slide’s conclusion: the future is not human versus AI but human plus AI. What GenAI cannot easily replace: trust, responsibility, professional judgement, understanding the customer, ethical decisions, human relationships and knowing the local context.

Risks of GenAI

Wrong information, generic output, bias, privacy issues, overdependence, lack of originality. Professional development means learning to check AI.

The dangerous habit

Prompt → copy → submit may give an answer but not a capable graduate. Better: prompt → understand → verify → adapt → create.

Using AI safely

Use it to speed up learning, not replace it. Check facts, understand the output, protect confidential information, and follow academic and professional rules.

From mindset to digital entrepreneurship

An entrepreneurial mindset is not “I must start a company”. It is: see a problem, find an opportunity, create value, test, improve. Employees can have it too. Digital entrepreneurship means creating value with digital technology: e-commerce, digital services, apps, websites, content, online education, data services, freelancing. Start small. GenAI lowers an old barrier (“I need a whole team”) because one person can prototype content, a website, a business idea, customer communication and data analysis. But a prototype is not a successful business.

The slides give two cases: a data graduate (Excel and Power BI skill; small businesses cannot understand their sales data; solution is a simple dashboard with monthly insights; GenAI helps explain trends and draft reports) and a computing graduate (web development skill; a small business has no simple booking system; solution is a low-cost booking website that saves the owner time).

Show evidence, not a list

Do not only say “I know Power BI.” Show it with a portfolio: dashboard, website, app, campaign, case study, data analysis, prototype or digital product. Evidence is stronger than a list of skills.

New graduate skill stack (7)
1 Domain knowledge · 2 Digital skills · 3 Communication · 4 Critical thinking · 5 Problem solving · 6 AI literacy · 7 Entrepreneurial mindset

The big connection (slide): professional development builds capability; GenAI amplifies it; an entrepreneurial mindset finds opportunities; digital entrepreneurship creates value. Take-home line: learn, build, show, solve, improve, use GenAI, but remain the human in charge.

Swipe sideways to see the whole map

Three levels: strategy, tactics, operations

The slides say every business, big or small, has two main uses of managers’ time: strategy and tactics, and a third part, operations, completes them.

Strategy

The long-term goals and the ways to reach them: where the company should go and how to succeed in the medium and long term.

Tactics

Actions and decisions that aim at specific goals and support the strategy; daily operations and controlling ongoing processes.

Operations

The routine tasks, methods, steps and resources needed to run the business every day and carry out the tactics.

The slides’ example: a large e-commerce firm sets a strategic goal to raise sales by 20% in Q4. Marketing then launches a targeted email campaign (tactical) and operations handles the extra orders (operational). Doing tasks without a clear goal is only productivity; the slides want efficiency, meaning results, so start from goals and work out which tasks are needed.

Do not be “number one” in everything

The owner should generate ideas and open new space for growth. An owner who does every job (management, accounting, driving the van) causes stagnation: stopping, with no growth or change. The slides advise delegating and hiring specialists, which looks costly but pays off in profit and effectiveness.

Stagnation and how firms escaped it

FirmPressureWhat it did
Zalora / FashionValetShopee and Lazada widened their products and customers.Strategic partnerships with payment gateways and logistics firms; invested in AI and machine learning for stock, recommendations and targeted marketing.
eBayCompetition from Amazon and AliExpress; trouble keeping buyers and sellers.Refocused on its core strength, a marketplace for unique, hard-to-find items; allowed new products next to used ones (C2C and B2C); improved its interface and search.
AmazonIts size and rivals such as Walmart, Alibaba and Shopify.Expanded beyond e-commerce (AWS cloud, Prime Video, Whole Foods, Amazon Go) and kept investing in customer experience, for example Prime membership.

The slides’ general cure: decisive action, new strategies, and promoting high self-organisation. The common thread is that an e-business must keep innovating.

Two tools for using time well

Pareto principle (80/20): most results come from a small share of the effort or sources. In strategic planning the slides suggest about 20% of time for mandatory strategic planning and 80% for everything else. Notice that the slide states the principle only; it gives no worked business examples like the ones in Exclusive Tips below.

Eisenhower matrix (important versus urgent). The slides turn it into a time split for a business: 20% of time for strategic tasks, 60% for routine tasks such as customer service and mail, and 20% for force majeure (unexpected events). Their example: a logistics firm thought force majeure took 50 to 60% of its time, but most of it was really routine work such as car repairs. Seeing that let them stop stressing, plan the routine, and hire a specialist for it.

The strategic framework

The long-term vision and roadmap that guides decisions in the digital world. Its focus is high-level goals: market position, value proposition, infrastructure planning and overall revenue strategy. It aims to line up all resources and activities for growth that lasts. Three key areas:

Business objectives

Growth targets (for example +30% online sales), value proposition (what makes you worth buying from; Zalora: trendy, affordable fashion with fast delivery) and market expansion (Amazon: books to groceries, electronics, media).

Infrastructure investment

Cloud (AWS, Google Cloud) copes with traffic spikes in big sales; e-commerce platforms (Shopify for fast launch, Magento for customising) plug into payments and stock.

Revenue operations (RevOps)

Aligns sales, marketing and customer service on one strategy, data and tools. Real-time analytics (HubSpot, Salesforce, Marketo) drive decisions. TikTok is the slides’ example.

The tactical framework

Turns strategic goals into practical steps using technology, customer insight and processes.

Key componentObjective and tools (from the slides)
Marketing campaignsConvert potential customers into loyal buyers. Targeted Facebook and Google ads, Instagram ads, personalised content. Zalora tailors ads for Black Friday and Chinese New Year from customer data.
CRMManage customer relationships through their whole life with the firm. HubSpot, Zoho; Salesforce for large firms; HubSpot CRM has a free option for small firms.
AutomationAutomate repetitive tasks so the team can focus on strategy. Zapier links apps (a web form feeds the CRM); email automation (Mailchimp, SendGrid, HubSpot) nurtures leads and follows up abandoned carts.
Integrating RevOpsMarketing, sales and support work together, with no silos and no bottleneck in the sales cycle. Marketing gives sales good leads, sales close them, support solves issues and feeds back to both.

The operational framework

Keeps daily activity running smoothly so the business meets customer demand efficiently, using the right tools and workflows.

Key componentObjective and tools
Order fulfilment and logisticsDeliver accurately, efficiently and on time. Couriers and tracking (ShipBob, UPS, J&T Express, DHL); AI route planning; order management systems (Brightpearl, Cin7) automate stock, orders and delivery updates.
Customer supportFast, reliable, personal help. Zendesk, LiveChat, Freshdesk; AI chatbots; IVR phone routing; self-service FAQs and knowledge bases.
RevOps executionLine up operational workflows with revenue activity to improve experience and retention. HubSpot Service Hub; Shopee and Lazada track support tickets and delivery feedback and spot upsell and cross-sell chances.

Technology and typical problems

Role of technology

Cloud (AWS, Azure) for flexibility; automation (Zapier, ActiveCampaign); real-time analytics (Google Analytics, Power BI, Tableau); RevOps platforms (HubSpot, Salesforce).

Challenges and fixes

Data silos: RevOps systems share data. Scaling: cloud scales up and down. Retention: AI and predictive analytics spot at-risk customers. Security: follow rules such as GDPR and use secure payment gateways such as Stripe.

For small start-ups the slides advise a niche market, affordable tools (Instagram Shops, Canva, Shopify) and customer feedback; a local example is a batik seller using Instagram Shops, Shopee Seller Center and Mailchimp, delivering with Pos or J&T Express. Established firms invest in data and RevOps; all firms keep customer satisfaction at the core and can use chatbots to support customers without growing cost.

Routine daily tasks, methods and resources that keep the business running.

Stopping or lacking growth, development or change. Harmful: dissatisfaction, low productivity, lost opportunities.

About 20% of effort gives about 80% of results; the other 80% of effort gives the remaining 20%.

RevOps (revenue operations)
Uniting sales, marketing and customer success around shared data, tools and processes to grow revenue.
Data silo
Data stuck in one team, so others cannot see it. The fix named in the slides is a RevOps system that shares data.
CRM

OMS

IVR

Churn
Customers leaving. The slides mention reducing churn by spotting at-risk customers.

Why revenue models come first

After you can run an e-business (Topic 3), the next question is how it earns money. A revenue model is how a company brings in money. The learning goals of the chapter are: what a revenue model is, how firms change theirs, the revenue issues of selling online, how to build a good web presence, what makes a site usable, and how firms use the web to connect with customers.

Five web revenue models

ModelIdea
Web catalogSell products from an online catalog, an online version of a mail-order catalog.
Digital contentSell or license information or media that is delivered over the web.
Advertising-supportedThe content is free to users and advertisers pay.
Advertising-subscription mixedUsers pay a subscription and ads also appear.
Fee-basedCharge for a transaction or a service.

The slide lists these five names and notes that the same model can work for both B2C (business to consumer) and B2B (business to business) sales. The one-line ideas in the table are my own plain-words reading, because the slides give only the names and pictures. The slides’ revenue-model picture adds ideas such as subscription, pay per view, commissions, affiliate, feature listings, ads and sponsorships.

Web catalog

Product catalog management means keeping product data high quality across all sales channels: organising, standardising and publishing it to each channel. The slides give four reasons to invest in it.

  • Organised catalog: customers cannot touch the product, so detailed descriptions and images plus easy search make up for it
  • Consistent information: no short forms or false claims, across all channels
  • Better customer experience, even for first-time buyers
  • Business growth: a base for a bigger catalog and a faster launch

The catalog “must have” features shown on a slide figure: sort and filter (colour, size, price, rating), quick view, a back-to-top button, and a way to load more products without paging.

Multi-channel versus omni-channel

Multi-channel

Promote and sell wherever customers buy: marketplaces, social media, messaging apps, online communities. Each channel works on its own.

Omni-channel

All channels (direct marketing, ads, sales, web, mobile, call centre) are fused into one approach with unified data and experience. It is about the customer. Example: an abandoned cart triggers an email coupon, retargeting ads remind of it, and a finished order brings cross-sells.

CharacteristicMulti-channelOmni-channel
Goals and incentivesEach channel has its own goalsIncentives drive collaboration across channels
Customer dataStored separately per channelOne integrated view of the customer
AnalyticsChannel-specific360-degree view of all touchpoints
Agent alignmentChannel-specificSupports several channels, alone or with other agents

Slide figures also claim that adding channels raises revenue (the chart shows average increases of 38%, 120% and 190% for one, two and three extra channels over cart-only) and that omni-channel firms keep far more customers (89% versus 33%). These are the figures as I read them from low-resolution images, so check before quoting.

Digital content, subscriptions, advertising

Digital content: freemium

Give a free tier and charge for a premium one. The slides use Spotify: an ad-supported free service plus paid premium. Other content types sold online: ebooks, music, courses, software, photos, templates.

Other digital content models

Fixed-fee sponsorships, subscriptions, cost-per-click advertising, and affiliate marketing and sales.

Affiliate marketing

Endorse a product you like on your site; when a visitor clicks your affiliate link and buys, you get a share of the sale. Quick and popular.

Subscriptions

Netflix, Spotify and Amazon Prime. Benefits: loyal user base, steady predictable revenue, predictable sales pipeline. Hard to build: these firms spend billions on original content to keep people renewing.

Advertising revenue

Why marketing comes after selling

A site with a good revenue model and design still earns nothing if nobody comes. The chapter’s goals: product-based and customer-based strategies, talking to market segments, customer characteristics along the relationship life cycle, web advertising, email marketing, technology-enabled CRM, brands on the web, viral social media campaigns, and search engine positioning.

Goals and the marketing mix

SMART goals

Specific (for example raise market share 10%), Measurable, Attainable (a stretch but not near impossible), Relevant to the mission, Time-based with a finish date such as one year.

4Ps, extended to 7Ps

Product (what you sell), Price, Promotion (how you talk to buyers), Place (where it is sold). The extended mix adds People, Process and Physical evidence.

StrategyProsCons
Product-basedFocus on what you do best; build a reputation for quality; loyalty to the product.You can leave customers behind; a quality product still has to match a target market.
Customer-basedFind out what customers want and provide it; more likely to buy; more loyalty.Costly (market research, hard for small firms); can distract from making quality products.

Segment your market

Divide customers into groups with shared qualities so each group gets a better, more fitting experience. The slides use four bases: geographic (country, city, climate), demographic (age, gender, income, education), psychographic (lifestyle, interests, values, personality) and behavioural (purchase, usage, occasion, loyalty, buyer stage). The flow is segmentation → targeting → positioning.

  • VIPs: shop most often or spend most
  • Active, long-time recurring users
  • Reactivated: bought, went quiet, bought again
  • Low-tier recurring: shop less and spend less
  • New customers (first purchase)
  • New visitors who joined the mailing list without buying

Why it pays, per the slides: saves time and money, builds better customer relationships, shows your strengths and weaknesses. One slide quotes a DMA finding of a 760% boost in email revenue from segmented campaigns; another shows an example where segments average $42 per customer against $28 for all customers (slide figures).

Relationship marketing and CRM

Relationship marketing is everything a brand does to build strong, lasting customer relationships (influencer programs, referrals, affiliate marketing), and the key part is two-way conversation. CRM aims to improve customer satisfaction, expand the customer base, enhance sales and improve workforce productivity. Its three functions, sales, marketing and customer service, run along the customer lifecycle and support each other.

The slide on return on investment (ROI) shows referral revenue growing about 3 times between year 1 and year 2, and says long-term partnerships are the best way to approach relationship marketing.

Advertising on the web and picking tactics

Display ad types

Text, image, video and mobile ads. Ads can be classed by how they are served (banner, contextual, search, local, mobile, map, social), how they are displayed (expandable, floating, pop-up, interstitial, wallpaper and others) and where (browser, non-browser, feeds, newsletters, email).

Seven uses of the Internet in marketing

Raise awareness; build loyalty; generate leads; shorten the sales cycle with relevant information; collect customer information; communicate company information; improve customer service.

Four adopter stages

Traditionalists (offline first, low risk), pragmatists (cautious trials, no plan or budget), adopters (use several tactics, small budget), innovators (full engagement, test new tactics such as video and mobile search).

Seven factors for selecting tactics

FactorAsk yourself
1. Online goalsMap goals to business objectives: awareness, visibility, traffic, online or offline sales, sign-ups, downloads.
2. Business environmentB2B has longer sales cycles (for example paid search leading to a whitepaper download, then email follow-up); a crowded market needs a standout idea.
3. Online audienceWho are they and where do they spend time online? Age, income, gender, media habits, web experience.
4. Online competitorsRivals include local and global firms and marketplaces; study their tactics and either compete head-on or find a niche.

Marketing process and email

The target marketing process moves people from strangers to visitors to leads to customers to promoters. A content marketing plan asks: who are your people, what are your objectives and goals, what content, how often, and how will you promote it.

Email marketing

The slides say email outperforms the other online strategies (SEO, pay-per-click, content marketing) and is the most cost-effective way to reach goals. Strategies: real-time personalisation, multi-channel marketing intelligence, scheduling campaigns, and strong content. Benefits chart: more leads, better sales, better conversion rates, lower marketing cost.

Email process: 5 steps

Define (name the campaign, subject, details) → test (browsers, email clients) → send (now or scheduled) → measure (opens, clicks, activity) → report (usage, bounces, social sharing).

Branding

A brand is more than a logo: it is the customer’s total experience, in your promise, values, personality and how you talk and answer the phone. It is a badge of trust. A good brand delivers a clear message, proves credibility, connects emotionally, motivates a purchase and creates loyalty. Loyal customers need yes to three questions: did it perform as expected, was quality as good as promised or better, and was the whole experience positive?

Branding (identity)

Brand purpose, personality, guidelines (visual rules, voice, tone) and brand experience (consistency builds trust).

Marketing (sharing it)

Informative (awareness), inspirational, profitable, a tool for selling. Do not sound too salesy or you lose credibility.

Checklist to evaluate a brand: does it relate to the audience at a glance, show your uniqueness, reflect your promise and hold value inside, and reflect the values you want to represent. Brand guidelines give marketing its outline; marketing then reinforces the brand.

Viral, search and the channel mix

Viral marketing

Users pass the message on, creating buzz. How: appealing content, the right platform for the audience, the right influencer. Methods: social media optimisation, SEO, influencers, mobile. Benefits: lower cost, huge reach, better interaction, awareness (good for new firms).

Search engine marketing

Organic rankings come from SEO. Paid search is pay-per-click: advertisers bid for a keyword and the highest bid gets the top spot. A slide cites paid search visits converting 35% better than organic for e-commerce.

SEO versus PPC

SEO: long-term return, high volume, awareness; but hard to measure, slow, needs design and development work. PPC: quick to set up, measurable; but costlier, lower ceiling, and can suffer ad blindness.

Owned, paid, earned

Owned: you control it (your site). Paid: you pay for placement (PPC, paid social). Earned: others give you free exposure. They work best together; the combined return exceeds the sum of the parts.

1Pareto A retailer finds that 20% of products cause 80% of returns…

A retailer finds that 20% of products cause 80% of returns. Using the Pareto Principle, the company should:

  1. a. Increase discounts on top problematic products and other products
  2. b. Investigate and improve the top problematic products
  3. c. Ignore the returns because they are a minority, focus on top perform products
  4. d. Remove all top problematic products from the catalog

Answer: b. Investigate and improve the top problematic products

Pareto says a small part of the cause (20% of products) gives most of the effect (80% of returns), so effort on that small part pays most. Fixing it is the efficient move. Removing the products (d) throws away sales, discounts (a) do not address the cause, and ignoring (c) wastes the lever. This question appears twice on the lecturer’s slides (once headed TIPS) with the options in a different order, so learn the content: “investigate and improve the top problematic products”.

Basis: Notes-backed: Topic 3 (Pareto, 20% effort gives 80% results). The slides state the principle; the application to returns is my reasoning.

A Malaysian YouTuber uploads clips from another creator’s video without asking permission, and then monetizes the video. Under the Malaysian Copyright Act 1987, what legal issue is most likely to arise from this action?

  1. a. Copyright infringement because the creator’s exclusive rights were used without consent
  2. b. Moral rights requiring attribution of the creator
  3. c. Any use of copyrighted content is allowed if it’s only on YouTube
  4. d. Fair dealing automatically covers all uses without consent

Answer: a. Copyright infringement because the creator’s exclusive rights were used without consent

Using someone else’s video without permission, then earning money from it, is the textbook case of copyright infringement. c and d use “any” and “automatically”, which are wrong. b is a real idea, but “most likely” points to infringement of the exclusive rights. Exam traps

  • The 20/60/20 time split (strategy, routine, force majeure) is not the same as the 80/20 Pareto rule.
  • Pareto says act on the vital few. It does not say to ignore the rest or remove the top 20%.
  • Multi-channel = many channels, each separate. Omni-channel = all joined with one customer view.
  • Freemium = free tier + paid premium. Subscription = recurring payment. Do not mix them up.
  • AdWords/Google Ads shows ads on search results. AdSense shows ads on your own website and pays you per click.
  • Organic search ranking comes from SEO; paid search is pay-per-click bidding.
  • Owned = your own property; paid = you pay; earned = others give free exposure.
  • 4Ps are product, price, promotion, place. People, process and physical evidence are the extra three of the 7Ps.
  • Product-based strategy can neglect customers; customer-based strategy can be costly.
  • RevOps joins sales, marketing and customer service. CRM manages the customer relationship.
  • Absolute words in options (always, guaranteed, automatically, ignore) are usually the trap.
  • Mini project engagement weight: guidelines deck says 20%, agenda document says 30%. Confirm with the lecturer.
